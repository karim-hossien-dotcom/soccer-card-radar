# Veriswap: how it works, data access, trade-up economics (researched 2026-11-12)

## Question and what "better" means here
Can Veriswap (veriswap.com, iOS id6443471946, Android com.veriswap.veriswapapp) help consolidate several mid
soccer cards into one bigger target for less than "sell on eBay, then buy", and can the app use Veriswap data?
"Better" for this owner means: used weekly, loses less money to fees and spread than the eBay route, and surfaces
counterparties who value our cards above their 90-day comps.

Research only. No account, no login, no contact, no trade proposed. All dates below are when each source was
checked: 2026-11-12. "Confirmed" = read on a primary source today. "Secondary" = third-party page. "Inferred" =
my reasoning, not a sourced fact.

Warning about a look-alike: `veriswap-dev.azurewebsites.net` (Terms, User Guide) is an unrelated currency-swap
service (passport KYC, Russian payment rails, Moscow courts). It is NOT the card company. Nothing from it is used here.

## 1. How Veriswap works

### Company
- Veriswap Inc., Los Altos CA (BBB profile); hiring in Mountain View CA (newsletter Oct 24 2025). App Store seller
  is "ASC Investments LLC", copyright "Veriswap Inc." Co-founder/CEO Raymond Li (press release Nov 20 2025;
  NoOffseason Mar 2024). Confirmed.
- iOS: 4.7 stars, ~4.1K ratings; latest 2.1.20 (Oct 26, Pokemon promo). Android: 10K+ installs, 4.4 stars,
  ~580 reviews, updated Sep 23 2026. Confirmed.

### Trade flow (help center "How is the process of trading", updated May 9 2025; confirmed)
1. Send or accept an offer (multi-card bundles, counteroffers, cash on either side).
2. Both confirm and pay Veriswap fees.
3. Each buys a label in-app (USPS, FedEx, UPS) and ships to a Veriswap hub. Neither trader ships to the other.
4. Hub waits for both packages, inspects that cards are authentic and match the terms.
5. Cards ship to the new owner or into the Veriswap Vault. Vaulted cards can be re-traded without shipping.

### Timelines (confirmed)
- Ship within 5 days of the trade; "majority ... ship within two business days" (help, updated Jul 8 2026).
  The penalty-policy article says ship within 2 business days to avoid a penalty (updated Aug 21 2025).
- Hub: authentication up to 48 hours (business days), aim to process within 3 business days of both arriving;
  outbound usually same day (help, updated May 9 2025).
- Reality per users (secondary, App Store reviews 2025-2026): 2 to 4 weeks door to door is common.

### Authentication and what is accepted
- Graders listed: PSA, BGS, BCCG, BVG, SGC, HGA, ISA, KSA, CSG, JSA, Fanatics, Arena Club, TAG (help, updated
  Sep 16 2025; confirmed). Android listing still says PSA, BGS, SGC, CGC.
- Raw cards allowed. Hub checks authenticity of raw cards but "does not guarantee" condition; raw trades are at
  the user's own risk (help "What does Veriswap authenticate", updated May 22 2025; confirmed).
- Contradiction (confirmed): the Terms (updated Jan 15 2025) §5 say "Veriswap does not authenticate cards"
  and leave authentication to the grading companies. The help center and app copy say the hub verifies both sides.
  Treat the Terms as the binding version: the hub checks contents match, not that a slab is genuine.

### Fees
- Terms §4: fees are "described in our FAQ, as updated from time to time" (confirmed). The current fee table
  (help article 38725384581915) could not be read today (open item 1).
- Loyalty tiers by quarterly volume: Base under $10K, Bronze $10-50K, Silver $50-200K, Gold $200K+; reset each
  quarter; 1,000 points = $1 trade credit (newsletter Jul 10 2025; confirmed). Graded fees adjusted Oct 13 2025
  (newsletter Oct 6 2025; confirmed, amounts not stated).
- Last published numbers I could find (secondary, blog Jun 13 2025, before the loyalty program and the Oct 2025
  change): trades 2% of declared value per side for sports, 3% non-sports and raw; Buy It Now / cash offers 3% per
  side sports, 4% non-sports; vault-to-vault trades 1.5% each.
- History (secondary, Elite Fourum May 15 2024): 1.5% (1.25% above $10K) raised to 4% per person.
- Cash in a trade: Stripe 2.9%, split equally between sender and receiver (help, updated May 9 2025; confirmed).
- Shipping: user buys the inbound label. User reports: about $5 for one card; $20 one way for three cards
  with insurance (App Store reviews; secondary).
- Insurance: inbound leg (you to hub) is NOT protected for transit damage, empty boxes, or non-Veriswap labels;
  outbound leg (hub to you) is (help "How are trades protected", Jun 9 2025; confirmed). A help title says
  "Why was my package insured for only $100" (not readable today).
- Fees non-refundable on cancellation (Terms §7; cancellations help, updated Sep 15 2026; confirmed).

### Cancellations and penalties (confirmed)
- Accepted trades are binding; cannot be cancelled by default (help, Sep 15 2026).
- At-fault returning traders go on probation: extra 2% "Accountability Fee" on every trade for up to 30 days.
  Suspension possible at a 25% cancellation rate with 12+ completed trades.
- Terms §8: penalty for failing to ship is "the greater of $200 or 80%" of the cards' value. This is the
  biggest single risk in the platform for us.
- Since Oct 2025, profiles show completed trades and trades the user "did not follow through on" (newsletter Oct 17 2025).

### Disputes
- Terms §8-9: "mediation if possible, otherwise through binding arbitration"; individual arbitration only.
  Liability limited to transactions in the past 30 days (§10). Vault: Veriswap pays "the lesser of market value
  ... or the user's declared value" (§9). Confirmed.
- BBB: 13 complaints in 3 years, all "Unanswered" in BBB's record; themes: card cracked at authentication, lost
  card with a low in-house valuation offer, wrong address, misleading market data in a trade (BBB page; confirmed
  that the complaints exist, their content is the complainants' claims).

### How "trade value" is set
- Traders set the prices: Terms §2 says valuations shown are "estimates only" and traders' prices "may be higher
  than the retail value" (confirmed).
- Fees are charged on the "declared value" (secondary, blog Jun 2025).
- Trade proposals show comparative values for each side (secondary, NoOffseason Mar 2024).
- App 2.1.17 (Aug 20 2026) "Added link to CardLadder comps" (confirmed, App Store release notes). So Veriswap
  links out to Card Ladder; it does not compute comps itself (inferred from the wording).
- Vault insurance: "Market value" is "determined solely ... by Veriswap valuation team" (Terms §9; confirmed).
- Public card pages show one dollar figure with no label; meta text says "available for sale or trade ... for
  $100 USD" (one Messi PSA 9 page; confirmed). That is an ask, not a sale.

Card-comps rule: a Veriswap figure is a listing ask or a declared trade value, never a comp. A reported Veriswap
cash sale with a date (e.g. 2017 Select Messi Kaboom PSA 10, $28,500, newsletter Oct 26 2025) is a sale, but it is
older than 90 days today, so context only.

## 3. User experience themes (App Store reviews, Android reviews, Elite Fourum, Mantel, BBB, blog)

Reddit, Blowout and X could not be read today (open item 2). Themes from what could be read:

1. Fees make small trades pointless. Users put the floor at about $100 (one review), $500 per side (Elite Fourum
   2024; App Store May 2025; Mantel Feb 2025). Example: $8 fee plus $5 label on a $10 trade; $70 on ~$1,000.
2. Slow: 2 to 4 weeks door to door; payments held "nearly a month" (one review).
3. Flakes: "more canceled deals than approved ones" (Dec 2025 review), fees not refunded when the other side
   cancels. Veriswap's answer: binding trades, probation fee, visible flake count (2025-2026).
4. Valuation fights: users list above comps; traders back out over "assigned value" (Mantel); one reviewer
   nearly accepted vintage OC slabs valued ~$400 that comped ~$200 each. Lowballing works the other way too:
   since values are user-set, the "trade value" shown is negotiating position, not market.
5. Raw cards: clearing for raw is "nearly nonexistent" (review); hub does not guarantee condition.
6. Support: slow email, hard-to-reach phone, BBB complaints unanswered.
7. Off-platform payment pressure from scammers (Dec 2025 review); Terms §2 says contract is between users.
8. Positive: one user did 13 trades worth ~$20K in a year and got cards they could not otherwise afford; works
   well for high-end graded slabs; community "trade nights" at shows.

## 5. Trade-up mechanics and economics

Mechanics (confirmed features): bundle 2-3 of our cards on one side, the target on the other, add cash to the
short side; counteroffer until accepted; hub swaps. Vaulting the result lets the next trade skip shipping.

Where value is gained or lost (inferred unless marked):
- Fees: Veriswap about 2% per side on declared value (secondary, mid-2025, may have changed) + inbound label +
  1.45% our half of Stripe on any cash we send. eBay: 13.25% final value fee on the total incl. shipping and tax up
  to $7,500, then 2.35%, plus $0.40 per order over $10 (eBay help page, confirmed today). The "about 13.25%" in
  the brief is right for trading cards.
- Spread: on eBay we sell at bid-ish (auction) and buy at ask (BIN). On Veriswap both sides name their own values,
  so the spread moves into negotiation.
- Consolidation premium: holders of one big card usually want more than face value for taking 3 smaller cards
  (more cards to resell). Veriswap users report asking above comps. If the counterparty demands more than about
  10% over comps, the eBay route wins (see benchmark below).
- PSA 10 premium: a counterparty that values a PSA 9 near PSA 10 money is the overvaluer we want. Check every card
  with the card-comps grade rules; never derive one grade from another.
- Raw cards: avoid as the incoming side (no condition guarantee).
- Risk: 2-4 weeks idle capital; flake penalty "greater of $200 or 80%" if we fail to ship; inbound leg not covered.

Illustrative benchmark (not a promise; made-up round numbers, not card values): three cards each comped at $400
for one target comped at $1,200, both sides at comps, no tax.

| Route | Cost | Net |
|---|---|---|
| eBay: sell 3, buy 1 | 3 x (13.25% x $400 + $0.40) = $160.20 fees; buyer pays shipping; plus sales tax and shipping on the buy | about $160 short of the target, plus tax |
| Veriswap 3-for-1 at comps | 2% x $1,200 = $24 (if fee is on our side's declared value) + one inbound label ($5-20) | about $30-45 short |
| Break-even | Veriswap loses once the counterparty extracts more than about $115-130 (~10%) over comps | |

Finding counterparties who overvalue what we hold (inferred method, manual):
1. For a card we own, read the asking prices of the same card listed by others (a few public card pages). An ask
   above our 90-day comp means at least one holder values it above market and may accept ours at their number.
2. Offer from strength: include the 90-day comp evidence (Card Ladder link is built into the app) for each card.
3. Prefer counterparties with many completed trades and zero flakes (shown on profile since Oct 2025).
4. Only trades where each side is at least $500 (fees and shipping floor reported by users).
